# SVP Copy Coach

Keeps Social Value Portal's written copy on-brand and free of AI tells. Use it as a background quality layer on any writing or editing task, not as a checklist you read back to the user. The user wants good copy, not a critique of their request.

If the task also involves **designed output** (slides, one-pagers, proposals, reports, infographics), read `svp-brand-guidelines` for colour, type and layout. This skill governs the words; that skill governs the look.

---

## How to use this skill

1. Write or edit the copy applying everything below by default.
2. Don't narrate the rules or explain what you removed unless asked. Just produce on-brand copy.
3. If something material is missing (audience, the one thing to land, the action you want), make a sensible assumption and state it in one line rather than stalling. Only ask if the gap genuinely blocks the work.
4. If the user's own draft already breaks a rule, fix it quietly in the rewrite. Flag a change only when it alters meaning or when they asked for feedback.

---

## Non-negotiables (apply to everything)

**British English, always.** organisation, programme, licence (noun) / license (verb), behaviour, recognise, optimise, centre, colour, whilst is fine but don't overuse it. Dates as 29 May 2026. Single quotes for quotes, double for quotes-within-quotes.

**Plain English.** Short sentences, simple structures, everyday words. Social Value is a technical space but the writing shouldn't feel inaccessible. Avoid jargon unless it's essential, and explain it on first use if you must use it.

**Active voice by default.** "Our platform measures your Social Value", not "Social Value is measured by our platform."

**Less is more.** Cut filler and long-winded phrasing. Say what needs saying and stop.

**Audience first.** Write about their world, their priorities, their outcomes, not ours. See the audience section below.

**Evidence over assertion.** SVP combats overclaiming and social washing as a founding principle. Back claims with proof points, results, benchmarks or case studies. Avoid empty superlatives, exaggeration and overpromising. Authority comes from credibility, not volume. If you don't have a proof point to hand, say so plainly rather than inventing a number — flag it as `[proof point needed]` so the writer can fill it.

**Inclusive and respectful.** No ableist, gendered or patronising language. "People experiencing homelessness", not "the homeless". "Underserved communities", not "the vulnerable". Write to include all identities and experiences.

## Avoid the AI tells

This is the heart of the skill. AI copy has recognisable tics. The problem isn't that people will know AI was used; it's that the copy reads as generic and uncared-for. Strip these out:

**Em-dashes used for emphasis.** Don't pepper prose with em-dashes—like this—for effect. Use commas, full stops, brackets or a colon instead. Em-dashes are acceptable only where genuinely needed grammatically, not as a rhythm crutch.

**The "it's not just X, it's Y" construction** and its cousins: "more than just a [noun], it's a [noun] that…", "X isn't about Y, it's about Z." These are the single most recognisable AI tell. Rewrite as a direct statement.

**Rule-of-three padding.** "Clear, consistent and human" can be fine, but don't force every list into a tricolon for rhythm. Vary list length. Two items is fine. Four is fine.

**Identikit sentence rhythm.** Vary sentence length and structure. If every sentence is the same shape, the piece is forgettable even when it's technically correct. Read it aloud in your head; if it drones, break it up.

**Tired buzzwords and B2B filler.** Cut or replace: fostering, cutting-edge, delve, synergy, unlock, future-proof, seamlessly, leverage, robust (when vague), elevate, harness, navigate (as metaphor), landscape (as metaphor), in today's fast-paced world, at the end of the day, game-changer, deep dive, holistic, tapestry, testament to, stands as, plays a pivotal/crucial role, underscores. None are banned outright, but they're AI-churned by default, so earn them.

**Over-capitalisation**, especially in lists and headers. Use sentence case (see naming below).

**Random emojis**, rockets most of all. Use an emoji only if a human writer at SVP genuinely would in that channel, not because it decorates.

**Hollow openers and closers.** Don't open with "In an era where…" or close with "Ultimately, it's clear that…". Get to the point and end on something concrete.

**False precision and hedging clichés.** Avoid "studies show" without a source, "it's worth noting that", "it's important to remember", "needless to say".

## Naming conventions (get these right every time)

- **Social Value TOM System™** on first reference in a document; **TOM System** thereafter. In titles, subheadings or graphics, "TOM System" is fine from the start.
- ✘ Never "National TOMs", "TOMs", or "the TOMs".
- ✘ Don't call the TOM System a *framework* in a procurement context, where "framework" has a specific procurement meaning.
- ✘ Don't call the platform "the Portal".
- Always capitalise **Social Value Portal** and **Social Value TOM System**, plus the sub-concepts **Themes, Outcomes and Measures**.
- Capitalise **Social Value** everywhere, including mid-sentence, to mark its significance: "Our platform captures Social Value across contracts, projects and teams."
- Keep impact, ESG, procurement and similar terms lowercase unless part of a proper noun or title.
- Spell out acronyms on first use: Social Value Portal (SVP), then SVP.
- If a client uses different terms, meet them where they are rather than seeming pedantic. Prefer SVP terms where you can do so without friction.

**Grammar and formatting:** sentence case for headings and most copy (easier to scan, less cluttered); use contractions (you're, they're, we've) for natural readability; ampersands are fine in headers and tight layouts but not in running prose; Oxford comma where it removes ambiguity; full stops on bullets only when the bullet is a complete sentence, not a fragment; be internally consistent within a single piece.

## Brand voice: ACCT

SVP's voice extends its values: **Accountability, Collaboration, Community, Transformation.** Hold the whole voice; don't pick one. SVP is the market leader in Social Value and writes with that confidence, while staying open: the field is still evolving and the tone reflects expertise *and* a willingness to keep learning.

- **Accountability** — evidence claims, avoid overclaiming and social washing, be cautious with superlatives. Show how and to what degree Social Value is delivered; don't just assert that it matters.
- **Collaboration** — lead without gatekeeping. SVP co-creates Social Value with clients, the impact sector and communities; it doesn't impose it. Open, respectful, partnership-minded. Helps dispel the myth that Social Value is a compliance exercise.
- **Community** — make Social Value real and accessible. Many readers are beginners (SVP's most popular resources are "Social Value 101" and "What is Social Value?"). Speak clearly about real-world impact, not just abstract numbers. Don't drift into detached, technical language.
- **Transformation** — sound visionary, because the mission is. SVP is reshaping how economies work for people and planet. Match that scale of ambition; flip the "corporate/compliance" perception toward radical, necessary change.

The throughline: **confident but empathetic, human, credible.**

## Audience tuning

Match the focus to who's reading. (Fuller detail in `references/audiences.md` if you need it.)

| Audience | Tone | Focus on |
|----------|------|----------|
| Government & NHS | Empathetic, aligned, purposeful | Community outcomes, policy delivery (Net Zero, Levelling Up, Missions), value for money; be an enabler, not a vendor |
| Suppliers to Gov | Supportive, clear, enabling | Winning bids, practical help, clarity; Social Value as competitive advantage, not "the rules" — but don't reduce their impact goals to pure commercial thinking |

Default when audience is unstated: confident, plain, human, audience-first. State the assumption you've made.

## Context tuning

The voice is constant; formality, depth and pace shift by channel.

- **Long-form thought leadership** — considered, structured, credible. A little more formal is fine, but formal isn't stiff or academic. Evidence and logic lead; empathy and storytelling make it land. Write with intention, not filler.
- **Presentations / slide copy** — lead with the idea you want remembered, not the script. One idea per slide. Compelling stats, simple visuals, bold takeaways. Don't let bullets do all the talking; the story unfolds in the delivery.
- **Social media** — short, sharp, personable. The shopfront. Personality and humour can shine, human voices matter. Reflect ACCT. Don't chase trends or use performative language.
- **Executive comms** — insightful, bold, individual. Reflects personal experience and vision. Can be elevated, exploratory, even provocative to spark useful debate. Don't shy from hard questions, but avoid anything that divides the movement.

## Messaging structures (reach for these in pitches, decks, outbound)

- **AIDA** (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) — introducing something new, winning hearts and minds fast.
- **PAS** (Problem, Agitation, Solution) — reframing a pain point as worth solving now.
- **FAB** (Feature, Advantage, Benefit) — explaining the value of a product or offer.
- **Before–After–Bridge** — showing transformation: the current pain, the ideal future, how SVP gets them there.

## The 60-second check before you hand it over

Run the draft against this mentally; don't print it back to the user.

- Does it reflect ACCT and put the audience's priorities first?
- Does it match the channel and context?
- Confident but empathetic? Plain English? Jargon explained or cut?
- Does it sound like something a human would say aloud?
- Is the key message easy to spot at a glance?
- Any AI tells left? (em-dashes for emphasis, "not just X but Y", forced tricolons, buzzwords, identikit rhythm)
- Are claims evidenced, or flagged where a proof point is missing?
- Would SVP be proud if this were the first thing a respected stakeholder saw?

# Appendix A — Audience profiles

Fuller detail behind the tuning table in SKILL.md. Read when you're writing something substantial for a specific audience and want to get the priorities and pressures right.

## Government & NHS — policy-driven, community-focused

**What they care about**
- Delivering policy outcomes effectively
- Aligning with government-wide objectives (Net Zero, Levelling Up, the government's Missions)
- Building stronger, trusted supply chain partnerships
- Supporting communities, especially underserved ones
- Managing limited budgets and demonstrating value for money

**Tone guidance**
- Reflect empathy for the pressures they face
- Emphasise alignment with *their* goals, not SVP's
- Be respectful, measured, focused on long-term outcomes
- Show SVP as an enabler, not just a vendor

## Government supply chain partners (suppliers to Gov) — competitive with clear goals

**What they care about**
- Winning more bids by standing out
- Navigating inconsistent Social Value requirements
- Building trust with buyers
- Managing cost and capacity (especially small businesses and social enterprises)
- Delivering on commitments

**Tone guidance**
- Offer practical advice and reassurance
- Frame Social Value as a competitive advantage, not "the rules"
- Acknowledge the challenges honestly
- Don't reduce their impact goals to purely commercial thinking

## Real estate & built environment — high-stakes, high-impact

**What they care about**
- Differentiating in a competitive, scrutinised market
- Securing planning permission and social licence to operate
- Demonstrating value to investors, regulators and communities
- Delivering projects that leave a legacy, not just structures
- Navigating complex, multi-stakeholder environments

**Tone guidance**
- Be commercially aware and opportunity-focused
- Show you understand their technical and operational realities
- Show how Social Value adds business value
- Position Social Value as a way to lead, not just comply

## Enterprise-scale businesses — global, complex, strategic

**What they care about**
- Navigating multiple ESG frameworks and requirements
- Managing large, complex supply chains
- Aligning sustainability with business growth
- Avoiding reputational and regulatory risk
- Driving innovation in sustainability strategy

**Tone guidance**
- Speak at a strategic, systems level
- Emphasise integration and forward-thinking
- Position Social Value as a unifying force in a crowded ESG field
- Acknowledge complexity, then offer clarity and confidence

# Appendix B — Before-and-after rewrites

Concrete examples of SVP copy fixes. The "before" lines are the kind of thing that comes out of an AI tool or a rushed draft; the "after" lines are on-brand.

## Killing the "not just X, it's Y" tell

**Before:** "The TOM System isn't just a measurement tool, it's a movement that's transforming how organisations think about impact."

**After:** "The TOM System gives organisations a credible, practical way to measure Social Value, and a shared standard the whole sector can build on."

## Cutting em-dashes used for emphasis

**Before:** "Our platform—built on years of sector expertise—helps you measure, manage and report Social Value—all in one place."

**After:** "Built on years of sector expertise, our platform helps you measure, manage and report Social Value in one place."

## Evidence over assertion

**Before:** "We're the undisputed leaders, delivering game-changing results for everyone we work with."

**After:** "Since 2014 we've helped members measure more than £56bn of Social Value across 250+ organisations." *(Pull live figures from svp-brand-guidelines; flag `[proof point needed]` if you don't have one.)*

## Audience-first rewrite (Government & NHS)

**Before:** "SVP's market-leading platform unlocks powerful Social Value insights through our cutting-edge TOM System."

**After:** "The Social Value TOM System helps you turn supplier commitments into measurable community outcomes, and show value for money to the people you answer to."

## Plain English and active voice

**Before:** "Value for money can be demonstrated by leveraging the comprehensive reporting functionality that is provided within the solution."

**After:** "Our reporting tools help you demonstrate value for money quickly."

## Buzzword strip

**Before:** "In today's fast-paced landscape, organisations must harness holistic, future-proof solutions to seamlessly navigate the ESG tapestry."

**After:** "ESG requirements are multiplying and often overlap. Social Value gives enterprise teams a single, consistent way to measure what their work delivers."

## Social post (short, sharp, personable, ACCT)

**Before:** "🚀 Excited to share that we are unlocking next-level synergies in the Social Value space! Stay tuned. 🚀"

**After:** "Most impact measurement tells you the what. The Social Value TOM System gives you the how."

## Naming fixes

- ✘ "We use the National TOMs to score bids." → ✔ "We use the Social Value TOM System to score bids."
- ✘ "Log in to the Portal." → ✔ "Log in to the platform."
- ✘ "the TOMs framework" (in a procurement doc) → ✔ "the TOM System"
